# Suspicious Wire Transfer Investigation
### Case Number: AML-2026-001
### Alert Type: Structuring / Suspicious International Wire Activity
### Status: Escalated for SAR Review
### Risk Rating: High

### Executive Summary

An automated transaction monitoring alert identified unusual activity involving a newly opened consumer checking account. Within 45 days of account opening, the customer made three cash deposits totaling $25,500 before initiating an international wire transfer of $24,000 to a beneficiary located in a high-risk jurisdiction.

The customer's account activity deviated significantly from the expected behavior established during onboarding and presented several money laundering red flags, including rapid movement of funds, large cash deposits, and international fund transfers inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation and anticipated account activity.

Following review, the activity was determined to warrant escalation for Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) consideration.

Investigation Summary

A review of account activity identified a pattern inconsistent with the customer's expected financial behavior.

Three large cash deposits were completed within a 17-day period. Shortly after the final deposit, the customer initiated an international wire transfer totaling $24,000 to an overseas recipient.

The account maintained minimal remaining balance following the transfer, indicating rapid movement of funds with limited account retention.

No prior domestic payroll deposits, business invoices, or recurring customer payments were observed that would reasonably support this transaction pattern.

Given the transactional behavior, reassessment of the customer risk profile is recommended.

---

Investigator Analysis

The transaction pattern raises concerns regarding potential layering of illicit funds.

The sequence of multiple cash deposits followed by a large international wire transfer shortly after account opening is inconsistent with the customer's declared income and expected account usage.

While each individual cash deposit falls below the Currency Transaction Report (CTR) reporting threshold, the cumulative pattern and rapid transfer of funds suggest potential structuring designed to avoid regulatory scrutiny.

The lack of supporting documentation explaining the overseas payment increases the overall risk profile.

Although no single indicator independently confirms money laundering, the combination of observed behaviors warrants further investigation.

---

Recommendation

Based on the available information, the following actions are recommended:

• Escalate the case to AML Investigations.

• Request supporting documentation regarding the source of cash deposits.

• Obtain documentation supporting the purpose of the international wire transfer.

• Reassess the customer's overall AML risk rating.

• Continue enhanced transaction monitoring for a minimum of 90 days.

• Consider filing a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) based on the cumulative suspicious activity observed.

Basis for Recommendation

The investigation identified several behavioral indicators commonly associated with potential money laundering:

- Rapid movement of funds shortly after account opening.
- Cash-intensive transactions inconsistent with customer profile.
- Large international wire transfer without documented business purpose.
- Activity inconsistent with expected account behavior.
- Multiple AML red flags observed within a compressed timeframe.

While additional customer outreach may provide clarification, the activity meets the institution's internal escalation criteria for SAR consideration.

Investigator Closing Notes

This case demonstrates the importance of evaluating transactional behavior within the context of the customer's expected financial profile rather than relying on individual transactions alone.

The investigation supports escalation for additional review while reinforcing the institution's obligation to monitor unusual activity, document investigative findings, and comply with applicable AML reporting requirements.
